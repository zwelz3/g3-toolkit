# Code of Conduct

## The standard

Be civil and be useful. Everyone participating here is doing so on top
of other obligations, so treat their attention as the scarce thing it
is.

That means, concretely:

- Criticize the code, the docs, or the decision, not the person who
  wrote it. "This breaks on an empty graph" is a review; "this is
  careless" is not.
- Bring evidence. A reproduction, a failing test, a measurement, or a
  citation moves a disagreement forward. Repetition and volume do not.
- Accept a decision once it is made, and say so plainly if you disagree
  with it. Reopening a settled question needs new information, not new
  phrasing.
- Assume the other person had a reason. Most surprising code in this
  repository has one, usually written down in a comment or a
  `planning/` record. Ask before concluding.

Not acceptable, in issues, pull requests, commit messages, or anywhere
else connected to this project:

- Harassment, insults, or demeaning comments, including about someone's
  identity, background, or level of experience.
- Sexualized language or imagery, and unwelcome attention of any kind.
- Publishing someone's private information without their permission.
- Sustained disruption: derailing threads, dogpiling a contributor, or
  reopening closed decisions to wear people down.

This applies to everyone, including maintainers. A maintainer being
right about the code does not license being unpleasant about it.

## Scope

Project spaces: the repository, its issues and pull requests, and any
space where someone is representing the project.

## Reporting

Report to the maintainer at <welz.zach@gmail.com>. Reports are handled
privately, and you will get an acknowledgement.

If your report is about the maintainer, and you would rather not send it
to them, GitHub's own abuse reporting is an escalation path outside this
project: <https://github.com/contact/report-abuse>. That is named here
deliberately, because a code of conduct whose only channel is the person
you are reporting is not a channel.

## What happens next

Proportionate to what happened, and stated when it is applied rather
than left implicit: a private correction, a public one, removal of the
offending content, a temporary block, or a permanent one. Anyone
enforcing this document who was involved in the incident should hand off
the decision.

## Attribution

this project's own, because a policy people actually read is worth more
than a policy people recognize.
